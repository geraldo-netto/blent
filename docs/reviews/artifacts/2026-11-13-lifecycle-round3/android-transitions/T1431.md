# T1431 — allow Disconnect during remembered USB retry

The retry policy now publishes its own read-only pending observation from
`RememberedConnection`. It spans the original backoff and restart admission,
including retry attempts that fail before authentication. `ConfiguredConnection`
forwards that exact owner's StateFlow; it does not infer retry eligibility or own
another timer. `ConnectionControls` keeps Disconnect enabled while this work is
pending, alongside its existing active/preparing cases.

The original wait also observes source cutoff. Disconnect, changed attempt/peer
or lost eligibility wakes it immediately rather than retaining the remaining
backoff. A final identity/eligibility check precedes restart, whose existing
configuration/admission boundary rechecks the connection state. `finally` clears
the pending observation after success, rejection, failure or owner cancellation.
Untouched and unproven-ended connections retain their disabled Disconnect control.

## Ownership and limits

One Activity-owned retry loop remains authoritative for its proven peer, attempt
and bounded 2–30 second retry policy. Its pending StateFlow contains only one
Boolean; it grants neither native admission nor retirement. The wait retains one
original target, one timeout job/timer and one source-state subscription, with no
added queue/history. Timeout is the scheduled retry boundary, not native release
or abandonment. Source cutoff and scope cancellation settle the original wait;
`finally` releases its presentation observation. There is no additional retry
producer or independent eligibility state in the UI/configuration adapter.

The Compose subscriber belongs to its containing composition. The retry loop
belongs to the existing Activity scope; destruction cancels it, while the
application-owned connection lane retains any original native worker. Native
replacement still waits for ResourceLane retirement and existing connection
access admission. No new native resource, reuse path, wrapping identity or
cleanup sponsor is introduced. Existing fake workers, virtual scheduler and
Compose rules settle their owned work during fixture cleanup.

All execution was hosted Robolectric/fake-worker verification. No tablet,
application installation, device settings, real USB process, deployment or
service was changed. Native Android/USB behavior is not established by these
results. The separate review report records the sole confirmed finding; no
additional actionable issue is left only in chat or temporary notes.
